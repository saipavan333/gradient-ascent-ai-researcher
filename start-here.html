Welcome — here’s how to go from zero to AI researcher.

This is a complete, self-paced path that builds every idea in modern AI from the ground up: intuition first, then the math with every symbol explained, then a from-scratch implementation you run yourself, then an experiment. You don’t need a degree — just curiosity and a little patience. This page shows you what you need, how the lessons work, the whole map, and a few routes through it.

Begin at Track 1 → Math & notation primer

What you need to start

Less than you think. Everything technical is taught in place.

✅ The honest prerequisites

Comfort with high-school algebra (moving symbols around, reading a graph) and willingness to run a little Python. That’s the real list.

You do not need university calculus, linear algebra, or statistics beforehand — those are built up from scratch inside the course, always starting from a picture. If a symbol ever looks unfamiliar, the Math & Notation primer decodes every one of them in plain English.

How every lesson works

The same “gold standard” shape each time, so you always know what to expect.

1
Intuition first. A vivid, everyday picture of the idea before any formula.
2
The math, gently. The equations — with every symbol named and explained the moment it appears.
3
An interactive widget. Drag a slider, press a button, and watch the idea move. Play with it until it clicks.
4
From-scratch code. A real implementation in plain NumPy you can run locally or open in Google Colab with one click.
5
Worked examples & common mistakes. Numbers carried all the way through, and the traps everyone falls into.
6
Quiz, practice & recap. Check yourself, stretch yourself, then a one-sentence summary you could say to a friend.

Your first week

A gentle way to build the habit without burning out.

🗓️ A relaxed starter plan

Day 1–2: Read this page, skim the notation primer, and do Track 1, Lesson 0. Play with the first widget.

Day 3–5: One lesson a day from Track 1. Run the code for at least one of them — locally or in Colab. Don’t rush; let each idea settle.

Day 6–7: Redo the quizzes without looking, and try one “stretch” practice problem. If you can explain a lesson to someone else, you’ve got it.
