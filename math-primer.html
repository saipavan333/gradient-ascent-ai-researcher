Math & notation primer

Every symbol, in plain English.

Formulas look scary only because the symbols are unfamiliar — each one is really just a short word. Here’s the whole alphabet the course uses, decoded. You don’t need to memorize this: skim it once, then come back whenever a squiggle looks strange. Inside every lesson, each symbol is also explained the moment it first appears.

Reading a formula

The little marks that change what a symbol means.

$x_i$
Subscript
Picks out one item — the i-th one.
$x_3$ is the 3rd entry of the list $x$.
$x^{2}$
Superscript / power
Usually a power (here, squared). Sometimes just a label.
$x^2 = x\times x$.
$\hat{y}$
Hat
An estimate or prediction — not the true value.
$\hat{y}$ is the model's guess for $y$.
$\bar{x}$
Bar
The average of the thing under it.
$\bar{x}$ = mean of all the $x$ values.
$\theta \leftarrow \theta-\alpha g$
Left arrow
Update: replace the left with the right.
One learning step.
$a := b$
Defined as
The left is defined to equal the right.
Sets up a definition.

Adding & multiplying many things

Compact ways to say ‘do this to every term’.

$\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$
Sigma — sum
Add up all the terms, from $i=1$ to $n$.
$\sum x_i = x_1+x_2+\dots+x_n$.
$\prod_{i} x_i$
Pi — product
Multiply all the terms together.
$\prod x_i = x_1\cdot x_2\cdots$

Calculus

How things change — the engine of learning.

$\tfrac{d}{dx}$
Derivative
The slope: how fast the output changes as $x$ changes.
Big slope = steep.
$\partial$
Partial derivative
The slope in one variable, holding the others fixed.
Used for multi-input functions.
$\nabla f$
Gradient (nabla)
The vector of all partial derivatives; points straight uphill.
Gradient descent walks the other way.
$\int$
Integral
The area under a curve — a continuous sum.
Total from adding up infinitely many slivers.

Sets & spaces

Where numbers and vectors live.

$\mathbb{R}$
Real numbers
All ordinary numbers, decimals included.
$3,\ -1.5,\ \pi$.
$\mathbb{R}^{n}$
n-dimensional space
A list of $n$ real numbers — a point or vector.
$\mathbb{R}^2$ = the flat plane.
$\in$
Element of
‘is in’ / ‘belongs to’.
$x \in \mathbb{R}$: $x$ is a real number.
$\{0,1\}$
Set
A collection of things, in braces.
Here, just 0 and 1.
$|S|$
Size of a set
How many items $S$ has.
$|\{a,b,c\}| = 3$.
$\subset$
Subset
Is contained within.
Cats $\subset$ animals.

Comparing & relating

Symbols that link two quantities.

$\approx$
Approximately equal
Close, but not exactly.
$\pi \approx 3.14$.
$\propto$
Proportional to
Grows in step with (up to a constant).
$y \propto x$: double $x$, double $y$.
$\le \;\; \ge$
Less / greater than or equal
At most / at least.
$x \ge 0$: $x$ is non-negative.
$\ll \;\; \gg$
Much less / much greater
One is tiny next to the other.
$0.001 \ll 1000$.
$\Rightarrow$
Implies
If the left holds, the right follows.
rain $\Rightarrow$ wet ground.
$\forall$
For all
Every one of them.
$\forall x$: ‘for every $x$’.
$\exists$
There exists
At least one.
$\exists x$: ‘for some $x$’.

Vectors & matrices

The objects ML actually computes with.

$\mathbf{v}$
Bold = vector
A list of numbers (a point/arrow), not one number.
$\mathbf{v}=(3,2)$.
$A^{\top}$
Transpose
Flip a matrix: rows become columns.
$A^\top$ reads $A$ sideways.
$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}$
Dot product
Multiply matching entries, add them up; measures alignment.
$0$ means perpendicular.
$\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert$
Norm / length
The length of a vector.
$\lVert(3,4)\rVert = 5$.
$A\mathbf{x}$
Matrix × vector
Apply the transformation $A$ to $\mathbf{x}$.
Rotate, scale, or shear it.

Probability & statistics

Reasoning under uncertainty.

$P(A)$
Probability
The chance of $A$, from 0 to 1.
$P=0.5$ is a coin flip.
$P(A\mid B)$
Conditional probability
The chance of $A$ given that $B$ happened.
‘$A$ given $B$’.
$\mathbb{E}[X]$
Expectation
The long-run average value of $X$.
Average payout of a bet.
$\mathrm{Var}(X)$
Variance
How spread out $X$ is.
Big variance = wide swings.
$X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})$
Distributed as
$X$ is drawn from this distribution (here a Gaussian).
$\mu$ = mean, $\sigma^2$ = spread.
$\arg\max_x f(x)$
Argmax
The $x$ that makes $f$ biggest — the winner, not the value.
argmin is the smallest.

Functions you’ll meet a lot

A few recurring building blocks.

$\log x$
Logarithm
Inverse of exponentiation; turns multiplying into adding.
Grows very slowly.
$e^{x}=\exp(x)$
Exponential
$e$ to the power $x$; rapid growth.
Undoes $\log$.
$\sigma(x)$
Sigmoid
Squashes any number into $(0,1)$ — a soft yes/no.
Used for probabilities.
$\mathrm{softmax}$
Softmax
Turns a list of scores into probabilities that sum to 1.
Picks a ‘soft’ winner.
$|x|$
Absolute value
Distance from zero (drop the minus sign).
$|-4| = 4$.
$\lfloor x \rfloor$
Floor
Round down to the nearest whole number.
$\lfloor 3.9 \rfloor = 3$.

Greek letters — the usual ML meanings

Same letters keep showing up; here’s what they usually stand for.

$\alpha$
alpha
Learning rate — how big each step is.
Also a generic constant.
$\gamma$
gamma
Discount factor in reinforcement learning.
How much the future counts.
$\epsilon$
epsilon
A tiny number, or an exploration rate.
‘a little bit’.
$\eta$
eta
Another symbol for the learning rate.
Same idea as $\alpha$.
$\theta$
theta
The model’s parameters (its weights).
What training adjusts.
$\lambda$
lambda
Regularization strength — or an eigenvalue.
Depends on context.
$\mu \;\; \sigma$
mu, sigma
Mean and standard deviation.
Center and spread.
$\pi$
pi
A policy (RL) — or the number 3.14159…
Context tells you which.
💡 One habit that makes math easy

Read a formula out loud as a sentence, replacing each symbol with its plain word. $\sum_{i} x_i$ becomes “add up all the $x$’s.” $\hat{y} \approx \theta^\top x$ becomes “my prediction is roughly the weights dotted with the input.” Once it’s a sentence, it’s just an idea.
