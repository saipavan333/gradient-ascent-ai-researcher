Activation Functions

The little nonlinearity inside every neuron — and the reason networks can learn curves at all. Each one bends its input differently, and its slope decides how gradients flow. Where the slope goes flat, learning stalls.

▸ Do this
Pick a function and slide the input. Watch the value and the slope (its derivative).
◈ You'll see
Sigmoid and tanh saturate at the ends — slope → 0, so gradients die. ReLU keeps a slope of 1 for positive inputs, which is why it trains deep nets so well.

What's happening: in backprop, each neuron multiplies the incoming gradient by its activation's slope. If that slope is near zero (a saturated sigmoid), the gradient shrinks and deep layers barely learn — the vanishing-gradient problem. ReLU's constant slope of 1 (for x>0) keeps gradients alive, which is why it became the default. This is Activations & initialization.
