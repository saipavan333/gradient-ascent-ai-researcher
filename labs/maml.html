Few-Shot Meta-Learning (MAML)

Ordinary training finds weights that are good at one task. Meta-learning finds a starting point that can become good at any new task after just one or two steps. It's learning how to learn fast.

▸ Do this
Press New task to sample a fresh sine wave (a new "task"), then Adapt (1 step) to fit it from the meta-start with just 5 example points.
◈ You'll see
From the meta-learned start (violet), a single gradient step (teal) snaps close to the true curve — because the start was trained to be one step away from every task.

Press New task, then Adapt — watch one step be enough.
What's happening: every task here is a sine wave with a random amplitude and phase. MAML searches for one initial set of weights such that, for any task, a single gradient step on a few examples lands near the true curve. So the meta-start isn't good at any one task — it's perfectly poised to become good at all of them fast. Compare with a random start: one step barely moves it. This is the essence of MAML / few-shot learning.
