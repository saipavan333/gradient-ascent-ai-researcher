Simpson's Paradox & Confounding

The most dangerous trap in data: a treatment can look harmful across everyone, yet help every single subgroup. The culprit is a hidden variable pulling the strings. Reveal it and the whole story flips.

▸ Do this
Press Reveal the hidden groups to colour the patients by severity.
◈ You'll see
The overall trend slopes down — treatment looks bad. But within each group it slopes up: treatment helps everyone. That reversal is the paradox.
x = treatment · y = recovery
—
What's happening: sicker patients were given more treatment and had worse baselines. That hidden "severity" is a confounder — it drives both how much treatment someone got and how well they recovered, faking a negative link. The fix is to compare like with like (adjust for the confounder), which is exactly what the do-operator and backdoor adjustment formalise. This is Confounding.
