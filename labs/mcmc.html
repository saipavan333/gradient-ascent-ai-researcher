MCMC: Sampling by Wandering

How do you draw samples from a probability distribution you can only evaluate, never invert? Take a random walk with one rule: steps that go "uphill" toward higher probability are always taken, downhill steps only sometimes. Amazingly, where the walker spends its time exactly matches the distribution.

▸ Do this
Press Walk (or Auto). The walker proposes a step; green = accepted, red = rejected. Samples pile into the histogram.
◈ You'll see
The histogram of visited positions grows to match the target curve — both humps — even though we never computed the distribution directly, only compared heights.

What's happening: we want samples from a target p(x) (here a two-humped mixture) but can only evaluate its height. Metropolis-Hastings: from the current x, propose a nearby x′; if p(x′) ≥ p(x) accept it, otherwise accept it only with probability p(x′)/p(x) (else stay put). Recording every position visited, the collection of samples converges to p — no normalization needed, since only ratios of heights appear. The step size is a Goldilocks knob: too small and the walker explores slowly; too large and most proposals are rejected. This is how Bayesian inference samples posteriors that have no closed form. This is MCMC.
