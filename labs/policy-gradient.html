Policy Gradient: Learning by Trying

Sometimes you can't compute the best action — you can only try things and notice what worked. Policy gradient methods like REINFORCE do exactly that: sample actions from a policy, then push the policy to make the actions that earned reward more likely.

▸ Do this
Press Learn (or Auto). The agent is aiming at a hidden target; each round it samples several shots and updates its aim.
◈ You'll see
The policy (the bell curve over aim angle) slides toward the target and sharpens — shots that scored high pull the mean their way, and confidence grows as it homes in.

Press Learn and watch the aim improve.
What's happening: the policy is a Gaussian over the aim angle, N(μ, σ). Each round we sample a batch of shots, score each by reward (how close to the target), and nudge μ and σ with the REINFORCE update: Δμ ∝ Σ (rᵢ − b)·(aᵢ − μ)/σ² — every sampled action is pulled toward or away from the mean in proportion to how much better than a baseline b its reward was. High-reward shots that landed to one side drag the mean that way; subtracting the baseline b cuts the noise. No model of the world, no gradient of the reward — just "do more of what worked", which is how policy gradients train robots and tune LLMs with RLHF. This is Policy gradients.
