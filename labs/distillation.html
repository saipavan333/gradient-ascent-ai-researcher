Knowledge Distillation

A hard label says "this is a dog." A big teacher model says "92% dog, 7% wolf, 1% cat" — and those extra numbers encode real knowledge about how classes relate. Distillation trains a small student to copy the teacher's full soft answer, so it learns far more than the one-word truth.

▸ Do this
Press Train student (or Auto). The student learns to match the teacher's soft distribution. Adjust the temperature that softens it.
◈ You'll see
The student's bars morph to match the teacher's — including the small "dog is a bit like wolf" mass the hard label throws away. That "dark knowledge" is what makes the student punch above its size.

Train the student to match the teacher.
What's happening: the teacher's raw scores are softened with a temperature T (dividing logits by T before softmax) to reveal the small probabilities the hard label flattens to zero — the relative similarities between classes, sometimes called "dark knowledge". The student minimizes the difference (a KL divergence) between its own softened output and the teacher's, instead of (or alongside) the one-hot label. Learning the full distribution gives the student a much richer training signal, so a small network can approach the accuracy of a big one — the trick behind compact deployable models like DistilBERT. Here the teacher is fixed and the student's logits are trained by gradient descent. This is Knowledge distillation.
