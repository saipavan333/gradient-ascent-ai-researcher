Local Minima & Momentum

Gradient descent always walks downhill — but "downhill" can trap you in a shallow dip while a much deeper valley sits just past the next ridge. Momentum gives the ball inertia, so it can roll through the ridge and find the better minimum.

▸ Do this
Release the ball from up the left slope, set a momentum, then press Drop. Two balls fall: plain gradient descent (grey) and momentum (gold).
◈ You'll see
Plain descent stops in the shallow left dip. With enough momentum, the gold ball carries its speed over the central ridge into the deep global valley on the right.
—plain GD — final height (lower is better)
—momentum — final height
grey ball = plain gradient descent · gold ball = with momentum · deeper valley wins
Set a start and press Drop.
What's happening: the landscape has two valleys — a shallow one on the left and the deep global one on the right — separated by a ridge. Plain gradient descent updates x ← x − η f′(x): it only moves downhill and halts the instant the slope is zero, so from the left slope it settles in the shallow dip. Momentum keeps a running velocity v ← β v − η f′(x), then x ← x + v, so it arrives at the ridge still moving and coasts over into the deep valley. This is why momentum (and Adam) train deep nets faster and escape bad flat spots — and why too little momentum leaves you stuck, too much overshoots. This is Momentum & optimizers.
