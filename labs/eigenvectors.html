Eigenvectors & Power Iteration

A matrix usually knocks a vector off its line — stretching and rotating it. But most matrices have a few special directions that only get stretched, never turned. Those are the eigenvectors, and finding the strongest one is how PCA, PageRank, and spectral methods actually work.

▸ Do this
Pick a matrix, then press Power step (or Auto) to repeatedly apply it to a starting arrow — from a random direction.
◈ You'll see
The moving arrow swings until it locks onto the dominant eigenvector (the white line) and then only changes length. The spiral matrix has no real eigenvector — it just keeps rotating.

Choose a matrix and press Power step.
What's happening: for a matrix M, an eigenvector v satisfies M v = λ v — applying M just scales v by a number λ (the eigenvalue), without rotating it. Power iteration starts from any arrow and repeatedly applies M and renormalizes; because the strongest eigen-direction grows fastest, the arrow converges to the dominant eigenvector, and λ is read off as the growth factor (the Rayleigh quotient vᵀMv). A rotation matrix has complex eigenvalues — no real fixed direction — so the arrow never settles. This is Eigenvectors & PCA.
