Weight Initialization & Signal Flow

Before a deep net learns anything, the random numbers you fill it with decide whether a signal survives the journey through its layers. Too small and it fades to nothing; too big and it blows up. The right scale keeps every layer's activations healthy — the whole idea behind He and Xavier initialization.

▸ Do this
Slide the init gain (how big the random weights are) and watch a signal's strength as it passes through 10 ReLU layers.
◈ You'll see
Below the He scale (√2) the bars shrink toward zero — the signal vanishes. Above it they grow without bound — it explodes. Right at √2 they stay level: signal preserved, deep training possible.

Slide the init gain and watch the signal.
What's happening: we push a random input (strength 1) through 10 fully-connected ReLU layers of width n, where each weight is drawn from a normal with standard deviation gain⁄√n, and we measure the signal strength (root-mean-square activation) at every layer. Each layer multiplies the signal's variance by roughly gain²⁄2 (the ÷2 because ReLU zeroes the negative half). So if gain < √2 the strength shrinks every layer until it vanishes (and gradients die); if gain > √2 it explodes; exactly at gain = √2 — He initialization — the variance is preserved layer to layer. (The tanh version of this is Xavier, gain ≈ 1.) This is why nets aren't just filled with small random numbers. This is initialization & signal propagation.
