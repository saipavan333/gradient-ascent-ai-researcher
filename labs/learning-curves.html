Learning Curves: Bias vs Variance from Data

When a model underperforms, should you gather more data or change the model? A learning curve — error plotted against how much training data you have — tells you which, by showing the tell-tale signatures of high bias versus high variance.

▸ Do this
Toggle a simple vs a flexible model and adjust the noise. Watch the training-error and test-error curves as the dataset grows left → right.
◈ You'll see
High bias (simple): both curves meet at a high error — more data won't help. High variance (flexible): a big gap between them that closes as data grows — more data will help.
—train–test gap at max data
—diagnosis
orange = training error · teal = test error · x-axis = number of training points
Toggle the model and watch the two curves.
What's happening: for each training-set size m we fit the model on m random points and measure error on the training set itself (orange) and on unseen data (teal), averaged over many draws. Training error starts near zero (easy to fit a few points) and rises as m grows; test error starts high and falls as the model sees more of the world; they converge. Where they converge tells you the problem: a high-bias model (too simple) converges to a high error and the gap is already small — adding data is futile, you need a richer model. A high-variance model (too flexible) leaves a wide gap that keeps closing — more data (or regularization) is exactly the fix. This is the practical face of the bias–variance tradeoff.
