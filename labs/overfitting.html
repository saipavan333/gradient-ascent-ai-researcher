Underfit, Fit, Overfit

The central tension in all of machine learning. Fit a curve that's too simple and it misses the pattern; too complex and it memorises the noise. Slide the model's flexibility and watch the sweet spot appear — and vanish.

▸ Do this
Slide the flexibility (polynomial degree) from 1 up to 12. Add new data to re-test.
◈ You'll see
Low degree → the curve is too stiff (both errors high). High degree → it wiggles through every dot (train error ~0) but the test error shoots up. Best is the middle.
—training error
—test error (unseen)
dots = training data · dashed = true pattern · line = model
—
What's happening: a degree-d polynomial is fit by least squares. Raising d lets it bend more, so it always fits the training dots better — but past a point it starts chasing the random noise, and its error on unseen data climbs. That U-shaped test curve is the bias–variance trade-off, and taming it is what regularisation and validation are for. This is Generalization.
