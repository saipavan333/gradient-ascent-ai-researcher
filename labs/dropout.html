Dropout: Training a Crowd of Networks

Dropout randomly switches off neurons on every training pass, so the network can never lean on any single unit — it's forced to build redundant, robust features. Cleverly, it's also training an exponential ensemble of sub-networks at once, and at test time their votes average back together.

▸ Do this
Set the drop rate and press Sample passes. Each pass keeps a random subset of hidden units (lit) and drops the rest (dim).
◈ You'll see
Every pass gives a slightly different masked output (gold dots), but their running average (gold line) homes in on the full network's answer (white). Dropout ≈ averaging a huge ensemble of sub-networks.

Press Sample passes and watch the average converge.
What's happening: during training each hidden unit is kept with probability 1−p and zeroed otherwise, so every pass runs a random sub-network. To keep the scale right, kept units are divided by (1−p) — "inverted dropout" — so the expected output is unchanged. That's exactly what you see: the noisy masked outputs (gold dots) scatter around, but their average converges to the full deterministic network (white line). Because there are 2ⁿ possible masks, dropout trains an astronomically large ensemble that shares weights, which fights overfitting and co-adaptation. At test time you simply use the full network (no dropping). This is Dropout.
