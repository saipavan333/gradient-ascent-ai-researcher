Convolution & Filters

The one operation behind every vision model. A tiny filter slides across your drawing and, at each spot, reports how strongly the local pattern matches it. Draw something, pick a filter, and watch the feature map light up on the right.

▸ Do this
Draw on the left grid (click & drag), then choose a filter from the menu.
◈ You'll see
The 3×3 window sweeps your image; the right grid glows teal where the filter fires and orange where it fires the opposite way — edges, blur, or sharpening.
left: your image · right: filter response
Draw a shape on the left. The window sweeping across shows convolution happening one patch at a time.
What's happening: at every position the filter multiplies its 3×3 numbers by the pixels underneath and adds them up — one number out. An edge filter sums to zero on flat areas and spikes at borders; a blur filter averages. Because the same tiny filter is reused everywhere, a CNN learns to spot a pattern anywhere in the image. This is Convolution, hands-on.
