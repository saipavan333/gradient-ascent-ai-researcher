SVD & Low-Rank Compression

Any matrix — including an image — can be rebuilt as a sum of simple layers, ordered from most important to least. The singular value decomposition finds those layers. Keep only the top few and you've compressed the image, keeping almost all of what your eye cares about.

Drag rank k from 1 upward and watch the reconstruction (right) rebuild from the most important layers first.
◈ You'll see
Just a handful of the 32 layers already make the picture recognizable — and you're storing a fraction of the numbers. The tall gold bars are the layers that carry the most.

Slide rank k and watch the picture reappear.
What's happening: the SVD writes a matrix A as A = Σ σi ui viᵀ — a weighted stack of rank-1 layers, where the singular value σi is that layer's importance (they come out sorted, largest first). Keeping the top k layers is the best possible rank-k approximation of the image (the Eckart–Young theorem). For a 32×32 image, k layers store k·(32+32+1) numbers instead of 32·32 = 1024 — real compression, and the error is exactly the energy in the σ's you dropped. Same idea powers PCA, latent factors, and model weight compression. This is SVD & PCA.
