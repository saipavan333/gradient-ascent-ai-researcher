Random Forest: Wisdom of the Trees

A single decision tree is powerful but twitchy — it carves the space into hard, jagged rectangles and overfits. A random forest grows many different trees on random slices of the data and lets them vote. The disagreements cancel out, leaving a smooth, robust boundary.

▸ Do this
Slide the number of trees from 1 upward and watch the decision region change.
◈ You'll see
One tree gives a blocky, over-confident boundary. As trees are added and averaged, the region softens into a smooth, sensible split — and the noisy islands vanish.

What's happening: each tree is trained on a bootstrap sample (the data resampled with replacement) and splits on a random subset of features, so every tree is different and makes different mistakes. To classify a point, all trees vote and we take the fraction — that averaging is bagging (bootstrap aggregating). A lone tree has low bias but high variance (jagged, overfit); averaging many decorrelated trees keeps the low bias while slashing the variance, which is why the boundary smooths out and generalizes better as you add trees.
