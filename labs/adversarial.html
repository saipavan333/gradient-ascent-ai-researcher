Adversarial Examples

A model can be 99% accurate and still be fooled by a nudge too small to see. Push a correctly-classified point a hair in just the right direction and it leaps across the boundary — same-looking input, opposite answer.

▸ Do this
Slide the perturbation ε from zero upward.
◈ You'll see
The point barely moves along the gradient arrow — then crosses the boundary and the prediction flips, even though the change is tiny.

What's happening: the attack moves the input along ∇xloss — the direction that most increases the model's error — by a tiny step ε. In high dimensions the decision boundary passes surprisingly close to normal inputs, so a minuscule, human-invisible shift is enough to cross it. Jailbreak prompts are the language-model version of the same trick. This is Adversarial Robustness.
