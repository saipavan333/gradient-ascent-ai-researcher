Entropy: Measuring Surprise

Information has a unit — the bit — and entropy counts how many you need. Reshape the distribution and watch uncertainty rise when outcomes are even, and collapse to zero when one becomes a sure thing.

▸ Do this
Drag the bars up and down to change how likely each outcome is (or use the skew slider).
◈ You'll see
Entropy is highest when all bars are equal (maximum uncertainty) and drops toward 0 as one bar dominates (no surprise left).

drag bars · height = probability
Drag a bar up to make that outcome more likely — watch the entropy fall.
What's happening: entropy is H = −Σ p·log₂p — the average surprise of an outcome, where a rare event (small p) carries more bits. A fair 6-way choice needs log₂6 ≈ 2.58 bits; a certain outcome needs 0. This is exactly the quantity a model minimises as cross-entropy loss, and the limit on how far data can be compressed. This is Entropy.
