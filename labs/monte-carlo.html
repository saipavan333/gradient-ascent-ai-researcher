Monte-Carlo: Estimating π by Chance

Some numbers are hard to compute directly but easy to estimate by throwing darts. Sprinkle random points in a square, count how many land inside a circle, and out pops π. This "guess with randomness" trick powers physics, finance, and reinforcement learning.

Press Throw (or Auto) to rain random points into the square. Watch the running estimate.

The fraction of points inside the quarter-circle, times 4, homes in on π ≈ 3.14159. More darts → less wobble (error shrinks like 1/√n).

Throw some darts to estimate π.
What's happening: the quarter-circle of radius 1 has area π/4; the unit square has area 1. So a random point lands inside the circle with probability exactly π/4. Throw n points, and (inside ÷ n) estimates π/4 — multiply by 4 for π. This is Monte-Carlo estimation: replace a hard calculation with an average over random samples. Its error falls like 1/√n, so each extra digit of accuracy costs ~100× more darts — slow, but it works in any number of dimensions where exact math is hopeless (which is why it's everywhere in simulation and RL). This is Probability in action.
