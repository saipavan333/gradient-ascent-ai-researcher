LoRA: Fine-Tuning on a Budget

Fully fine-tuning a giant model means updating every one of its billions of weights. LoRA's insight: the change you need is usually low-rank, so you can express it as the product of two skinny matrices — training a tiny fraction of the parameters while freezing the original model.

▸ Do this
Slide the rank r. The full weight-update ΔW (left) is approximated by B·A (right), built from two thin matrices.
◈ You'll see
Even a small rank reproduces ΔW closely — while training only 2·d·r numbers instead of d². The parameter count plummets with barely any loss.

Slide the rank and watch B·A approximate ΔW.
What's happening: instead of learning a full d×d weight update ΔW (that's d² parameters), LoRA freezes the pretrained weights and learns ΔW ≈ B·A, where B is d×r and A is r×d with a small rank r — only 2·d·r parameters. Here we take a target update and show its best rank-r factorization (via its SVD): even r = 2 or 4 captures most of a d = 32 matrix, cutting trainable parameters by an order of magnitude. This works because fine-tuning nudges a model in a few dominant "directions", so the update genuinely lives in a low-rank subspace. The payoff is huge: you can adapt a frozen billion-parameter model by training megabytes, and swap LoRA adapters in and out. This is Parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
