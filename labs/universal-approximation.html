Universal Approximation

The universal approximation theorem says a neural network with enough hidden units can fit any continuous function. The mechanism is beautifully concrete: each ReLU neuron contributes one "kink", and stacking enough kinks bends a straight line into any shape you want.

Slide the number of hidden units (ReLU kinks) and watch the network's fit chase the wiggly target.
◈ You'll see
A few units give a crude piecewise-linear sketch; add more and the fit hugs the curve. Each unit adds exactly one bend — the faint lines are the individual ReLUs being summed.

What's happening: the network is f(x) = w₀ + w·x + Σₖ wₖ · relu(x − tₖ), a sum of K ReLU units with kinks placed across the input range, and we solve for the output weights by least squares to best match the target. Each relu(x − tₖ) is flat then rises, so adding it bends the output at tₖ — with K kinks you get a piecewise-linear curve of K+1 segments that can approximate any continuous shape as closely as you like by increasing K. That's the universal approximation theorem made visible: depth and width buy you expressive power, and "enough neurons" really can fit anything (learning the right weights is the separate, harder story).
