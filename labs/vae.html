VAE: Walking the Latent Space

A variational autoencoder squeezes data down to a few numbers — a latent code — then decodes them back. The magic is that the code space is smooth: glide the point around and the decoded output morphs continuously, letting you generate brand-new samples.

▸ Do this
Drag the white dot around the latent map on the left.
◈ You'll see
The big decoded face on the right morphs smoothly — nearby codes give similar faces, and every point in between is a valid new sample.
left: 2-D latent space (drag) · right: decoded output
Drag around the latent grid — watch the face morph from sad to happy, wide to narrow eyes.
What's happening: the encoder maps each input to a point in this 2-D space and the decoder maps points back to outputs. Training with a smoothness pressure (a KL term pulling codes toward a simple Gaussian) makes the space continuous and gap-free — so you can sample any point and decode a plausible new sample, and interpolate between two inputs by walking the straight line between their codes. This is Variational Autoencoders.
