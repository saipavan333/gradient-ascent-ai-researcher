PCA: Finding the Grain

Principal Component Analysis finds the directions your data actually spreads along. The long gold arrow is the axis of most variance — squash everything onto it and you've compressed 2D into 1D while losing the least.

▸ Do this
Drag the spread and tilt of the cloud, then press Project to collapse it onto the top component.
◈ You'll see
The gold arrow (PC1) always lines up with the longest direction of the cloud; the shorter teal arrow (PC2) is perpendicular. Projecting keeps most of the shape in one dimension.

What's happening: PCA computes the covariance matrix of the points and takes its eigenvectors — the axes along which variance is largest. Keeping only the top few components is how you compress data, denoise it, or visualise something high-dimensional in 2D, losing as little spread as possible. This is Eigenvectors put to work.
