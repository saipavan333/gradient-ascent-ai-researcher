Top-k & Nucleus (Top-p) Sampling

Temperature reshapes the whole distribution; truncation sampling instead chops off the unlikely tail before sampling, so the model never blurts out something absurd. Top-k keeps a fixed number of candidates; top-p (nucleus) keeps just enough to cover a probability budget — adapting to how confident the model is.

▸ Do this
Switch between top-k and top-p and move the slider. The kept tokens light up; the rest are cut and the remainder is renormalized.
◈ You'll see
Top-k always keeps the same count. Top-p keeps few tokens when the model is confident and many when it's unsure — the adaptive behavior that makes nucleus sampling the default.

What's happening: the model outputs a probability for every possible next token (sorted tallest-first here). Top-k keeps the k most-likely tokens and zeroes the rest. Top-p / nucleus instead walks down the sorted list adding probabilities until the running total reaches p, then keeps exactly that set — the "nucleus". Either way the survivors are renormalized to sum to 1 and one is sampled. The key difference: top-k's count is fixed regardless of shape, so it can keep junk when the model is unsure or cut good options when it's confident; top-p adapts its count to the distribution, which is why it (often with a little temperature) is the standard decoding recipe.
