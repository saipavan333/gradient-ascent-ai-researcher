SVM: The Widest Street

Many lines can separate two classes — but which is best? The support vector machine picks the one that carves the widest possible street between them. Only a few points, the ones touching the curb, actually decide where it goes; the rest don't matter at all.

Press Train to find the maximum-margin boundary.
What's happening: the SVM finds the weights w, b that maximize the margin — the gap between the boundary w·x+b=0 and the nearest point of either class — while keeping the classes on their correct sides. Equivalently it minimizes ½‖w‖² subject to yᵢ(w·xᵢ+b) ≥ 1 (a wider street means a smaller ‖w‖). The optimum is pinned by just the points sitting on the margin lines — the support vectors; delete any other point and the solution is unchanged. Maximizing the margin is what gives SVMs their strong generalization, and swapping the dot product for a kernel lets the same idea carve curved boundaries. Here we optimize the soft-margin objective by sub-gradient descent. This is Support Vector Machines.
